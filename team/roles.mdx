## The roles

| Role | Who | Sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Client** | A customer with a login | Their own portal and content |
| **Setter** | Books calls by message | Their own leads and conversations |
| **Caller** | Books calls by phone | Their own leads and the fresh-lead queue |
| **Closer** | Takes calls, sells | Contacts assigned to them, their appointments |
| **Coach** | Delivers the service | Their clients |
| **Manager** | Runs the team | Everyone's contacts, Team, Meta Ads, Funnels, Workflows |
| **Owner** | Owns the business | Everything, including Finance, Brain and settings |

## Seats carry access

A person's access comes from the seat they hold, not from the role word. A Call Setter seat, for example, can read and edit contacts and appointments. Owners adjust seats under **Team**, **Settings**.

## Two hats

Someone can hold a second role. A closer who also sets sees both their closer contacts and their setter leads, and their numbers count in both places.

## Team numbers

Everyone can see team totals in Sales. Per-person breakdowns are for managers and owners. This is the one place where scoping is relaxed on purpose: the team should see the team's scoreboard.

## ARC and roles

ARC answers within your scope. Ask "how many calls did we book today" as a setter and you get your own. Ask as a manager and you get the team.

Some ARC surfaces are owner only: Brain, Thoughts, Autopilot and Brain Pack.

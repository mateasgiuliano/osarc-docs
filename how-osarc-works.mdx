## One brain, many apps

Most software makes you glue tools together. OSARC does not. Every app you install plugs into the same database and the same AI. ARC can read and act across all of them from the first minute.

When you open an app, you are opening a tool ARC already knows how to use. When you ask ARC a question, it reaches for the right app on its own.

## One record per person

A contact is the spine. Everything that happens to a person attaches to their record: the ad they clicked, the form they filled, the call they booked, every WhatsApp message, every payment.

That is why a contact card can tell the whole story, and why ARC can answer "what happened with this person" without you opening five apps.

## Your business, your scope

OSARC separates businesses completely. If you belong to more than one, use the brand switcher in the top bar. You only ever see the business you are in.

Inside a business, your role decides what you see. A setter sees their own leads. A manager sees the whole team. See [Roles](/team/roles).

## ARC proposes, you approve

ARC never changes data on its own. When you ask it to do something, it drafts a proposal that names the change. You approve it or dismiss it. See [Proposals](/arc/proposals).

## Words that matter

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Opportunity, deal, transaction">
    An **opportunity** is the card in a pipeline: the sales process. A **deal** is a package that was sold. A **transaction** is one movement of money. One opportunity can hold many deals, and one deal many transactions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Skill, workflow, hook">
    A **skill** is something an app can do for ARC. A **workflow** is a multi-step automation you build in the Workflows app. A **hook** runs when an event fires. They look similar and are not the same thing.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Set, show, booked call">
    A **set** is a call a setter booked. A **show** means the person attended. A **booked call** is any appointment on the calendar, whoever booked it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

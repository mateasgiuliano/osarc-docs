In a multi-tool stack, attribution breaks quietly and often. A Zap fires too slowly and a UTM parameter doesn't make it into the CRM. A lead books through Calendly and the source tag is lost in transit. Someone switches from one ad tracking tool to another mid-month and the historical data doesn't line up. By the time you're trying to figure out which campaign drove last month's revenue, you're already working with incomplete data — and the decisions you make from it are only as good as the gaps you don't know about. OSARC solves this by tracking the entire customer journey natively, in one system, with no manual steps and no external dependencies.

## The Full Attribution Journey

OSARC records data at every stage of your funnel, creating an unbroken chain from the moment a lead first sees your ad to the moment cash is collected.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ad Click">
    OSARC captures the UTM parameters, ad ID, creative ID, and traffic source at the moment the lead clicks through. This data is tied to the lead record immediately and never stripped or lost in transit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Landing Page & VSL">
    OSARC tracks time-on-page, VSL engagement by minute, and scroll depth on your landing page. You can see exactly where leads drop off in your video and how VSL watch time correlates with booking rate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Application Form">
    When a lead submits your application form, every answer is stored against their lead record alongside the originating ad data. OSARC can later surface show rate and close rate broken down by any individual form answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Booking">
    When a lead books a call through OSARC's native scheduler, the appointment is created instantly within the same system — no calendar webhook, no third-party sync required. Speed to lead (the time between form submission and first contact) begins tracking immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Setter Call">
    The setter claims the lead, dials from OSARC's native dialer, and the call is recorded and transcribed automatically. The disposition they log — whether the lead is qualified, rescheduled, or disqualified — is written to the same lead record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Closer Call">
    The closer takes the booked call. The call is recorded and transcribed. The disposition — shown, no-showed, closed, not closed — is logged on the spot and immediately updates the lead's attribution chain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Payment">
    When a payment is collected, OSARC records the cash amount against the lead record — which still carries the original ad click data from step one. Every revenue event is traceable back to its source creative, campaign, and funnel step.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Cash-Based Meta Pixel Events

Most businesses fire Meta pixel events on form fills and thank-you page views — which tells Meta's algorithm what a form-filler looks like, not what a buyer looks like. OSARC fires pixel events back to Meta on the events that actually matter to your business: shows, closes, and graded leads.

This means Meta's algorithm learns from real revenue signals rather than vanity metrics. Over time, your campaigns optimise toward leads who pay — not leads who click or fill in a form and disappear.

<Note>
  Cash-based pixel events are one of the highest-leverage changes you can make to a paid traffic campaign. When Meta's algorithm is trained on buyers rather than form fills, your cost per acquisition drops and lead quality improves — without changing your creative or your budget.
</Note>

OSARC handles this automatically. You do not need to set up custom conversions, write conversion API code, or manually map funnel events to pixel triggers. Every show, close, and qualified lead fires the right event to Meta the moment the disposition is logged.

<Warning>
  If you switch off OSARC's native tracking and use a third-party pixel, cash-based attribution events will stop firing to Meta. Your ad optimisation will revert to form-fill signals, and the attribution data in your OSARC reports will no longer match what Meta reports.
</Warning>

## What You Can Ask From Your Attribution Data

Because every touchpoint is recorded in one place, your attribution data can answer questions that a multi-tool stack simply cannot. Here are examples of the reports your data makes possible:

- **Which ad creative generated the most cash collected?** Compare revenue per creative, not just clicks or cost per lead.
- **Which funnel step has the highest drop-off?** Identify whether you're losing leads at the VSL, the form, the booking page, or between booking and call.
- **What is the booking-to-show rate per traffic source?** Understand whether certain ad platforms or campaigns send leads who book but never show — before you scale spend.
- **What is the close rate per setter?** Know exactly which setters are booking calls that go on to close, not just which setters are booking the most calls.
- **What did the leads who bought in the last 30 days have in common?** Surface patterns across form answers, traffic sources, and funnel paths for the leads who actually converted to cash.

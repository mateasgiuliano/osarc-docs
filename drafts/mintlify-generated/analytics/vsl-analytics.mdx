OSARC's VSL analytics go beyond simple view counts. Because your video player, application form, and CRM share a database, you can see not just how many people watched — but how much of the video the people who booked (and the people who closed) actually watched.

## What VSL Analytics Tracks

OSARC records granular engagement data for every viewer session on your Video Sales Letter. The following data points are available for every version of every VSL you host inside OSARC:

- **Total plays** — the number of times the video was started
- **Unique views** — distinct visitors who played the video, de-duplicated by session
- **Average watch percentage** — the mean proportion of the video watched across all sessions
- **Per-minute retention curve** — a drop-off chart showing what percentage of viewers are still watching at each minute mark
- **Watch time segment for leads who booked** — the average watch percentage among leads who went on to submit an application and book a call
- **Watch time segment for leads who closed** — the average watch percentage among leads who booked and then bought

The combination of these data points tells you not just how your video performs as a media asset, but how it performs as a sales tool.

---

## Reading the Drop-Off Chart

The retention chart plots the percentage of viewers still watching on the vertical axis against each minute of the video on the horizontal axis. A perfectly retained audience would produce a flat horizontal line; what you will typically see is a gradual decline with occasional sharp drops at specific moments.

**What the shape of the curve tells you:**

- A **sharp drop in the first 60 seconds** indicates a weak hook. Viewers are not finding immediate relevance or intrigue in the opening of the video.
- A **gradual steady decline** throughout is normal. Some attrition at every minute is expected — the question is whether the decline is proportionate.
- A **sharp drop after the offer reveal** suggests the pricing, framing, or risk reversal is not landing. Viewers were engaged up to that point, then lost confidence.
- A **plateau after a certain minute** indicates that the viewers who reach that point are highly committed — this is often around where a strong credibility or proof section appears.

**Comparing booked leads vs. all leads:**

The chart overlays two curves: one for all viewers, and one for viewers who went on to book a call. The point where the "booked" curve diverges upward from the "all viewers" curve marks the minimum watch time threshold for generating booking intent. Leads who drop off before this point are unlikely to apply regardless of what happens on the form page.

Use this insight to decide how much of the VSL you need to protect. If the divergence point is at minute 12 of a 25-minute video, keeping viewers past the 12-minute mark is your most important retention goal — not keeping them to the end.

## Using VSL Data to Improve Your Funnel

Use the accordions below to diagnose common patterns in your VSL data and identify the right lever to pull.

<Accordion title="Drop-off spikes in the first 60 seconds">
  Your hook isn't landing. The opening of your VSL is not giving viewers a compelling enough reason to keep watching.

  **What to do:** Rewrite the opening to lead with the biggest, most specific problem you solve. Name the problem before you name yourself or your offer. A strong hook makes the viewer feel that the next 20 minutes are directly relevant to their situation right now.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Drop-off after the offer reveal">
  Viewers were engaged through the education and credibility sections, but something about the offer presentation caused them to disengage. Price anchoring or offer framing may need work.

  **What to do:** Test a stronger risk reversal positioned immediately after the price reveal. Consider reordering the offer stack to lead with the most compelling deliverable rather than the most logical one. Review whether your price anchor (the "value" you assign to each component) is believable given your audience's frame of reference.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Closers outperform regardless of watch time">
  Your closer call results are strong even for leads with low VSL watch time. This suggests your application form is doing the qualification work that the video would otherwise do.

  **What to do:** Protect your form. Do not simplify or shorten it in pursuit of a higher application rate — the friction is filtering in your best prospects. You can afford to experiment more aggressively with the VSL script because the form is acting as a safety net.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="High watch time but low booking rate">
  Viewers are watching a significant portion of your VSL but not converting to applications or bookings. The video is holding attention but not driving action.

  **What to do:** The bottleneck is at the CTA placement or on the form step itself. Check whether your call-to-action appears clearly and prominently at the right moment in the video. Then review your application form for friction: too many fields, a confusing layout, or a headline that does not match the promise made in the VSL can all cause drop-off between a motivated viewer and a completed application.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  If you update your VSL, OSARC creates a new version entry automatically so historical data for the previous version is preserved. You can compare the drop-off curve, booking rate, and close rate between versions side by side — making it straightforward to confirm whether a script or production change is a genuine improvement before fully committing to it.
</Tip>
